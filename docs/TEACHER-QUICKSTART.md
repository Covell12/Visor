# Visor Classroom: teacher quickstart

One page to get your first class writing in Visor.

## 1. Make a class (2 minutes)

1. Sign in at your school's Visor address and choose **Create class** on Home ("English 10 – Period 3").
2. **If you've set your school** (in **Profile settings**), click **Add people** at the top of the class's page, tick your students (and any teachers to share the class with) in the list, and click **Add**. Only people who chose the same school are listed; schools aren't checked, so anyone who picks your school can see that list. Otherwise, or for anyone not on the list, students join with the **class code** (at the top of the class's page if you haven't set a school, and always under **People → Add people**; put it on the board), or use **Add people → Add students by email…** to paste their email addresses. Anyone without an account yet gets an invite and joins as soon as they sign up with that email.
3. On **People**, give extra time to students who need it (1.25×, 1.5×, 2×…). It's applied to every timed session automatically.

When everyone's in, use **Add people → Close joining with the code** so the code stops working.

**Teachers and advisors.** **Add people → Add a teacher or advisor…** shares the class with another Visor teacher account, in one of three roles. All of them can do everything you can (assignments, announcements, people, live sessions, review) except delete the class, which only its owner (whoever made it) can do.

- **Co-teacher:** listed as a teacher, and can add and remove teachers and advisors.
- **Advisor:** the same as a co-teacher, but not listed as a teacher, so students don't see them on the class. Anything an advisor posts still shows their name.
- **Assistant teacher:** listed as a teacher, but can't add or remove teachers or advisors.

Nobody can remove the owner, and anyone but the owner can leave a class themselves.

Every class gets a colour and a banner, shown on its tile on Home, in the sidebar and on the **Calendar**. Change them with the pencil at the top right of the class's page (or **Settings → Change background and colour**): keep the colour, pick one of eight designs, or upload your own picture (PNG, JPEG or WebP) and choose which part shows. Everyone in the class sees it.

## 2. Make a writing assignment

On the class's **Assignments** page, **New → Writing assignment**, then pick a preset:

- **In-class essay (locked):** 45 minutes, runs in full screen (a student who leaves full screen or leaves Visor sees only a warning, with their essay wiped from the screen, until they go back, and you see it on the Live tab), needs the Visor Chrome extension, students can only paste text from their own essay, AI sites are blocked, and it submits itself when time runs out.
- **Homework essay (open):** no time limit, pasting allowed, research recorded, AI sites blocked.

AI sites are blocked by default in both (untick **Block AI sites** to only record visits). Blocking works through the Visor extension, so in an open session a student without the extension isn't blocked; tick **Require the Visor extension** if every student must have it.

Change anything you like (time limit, the window when sessions can start, allowed sites, citation style, document style, Works Cited, word limits, starter text); the **Preview** beside the form shows what students will see as you go. Then **Save and publish**. Later, change any setting straight from the assignment's page: each control in **Session settings** saves as soon as you change it (a session that's already running keeps its time limit and sites until it ends). **Document style** sets how a new essay looks: an MLA essay (Times New Roman 12, double-spaced) or Docs default (Arial 11, 1.15 spacing). Students write in a Google-Docs-style editor with menus, a toolbar, pictures, tables, find and replace and an outline, and everything they do is recorded as before.

**Folders.** **New → Folder** makes a folder (a unit, a week) with a color and an optional description; folders can hold folders, three deep. Drag rows to reorder them, drop a row onto a folder (or onto a folder name in the breadcrumb) to move it, or use a row's gear menu: **Move to…**, **Move up**, **Move down**. Deleting a folder never deletes what's in it: its contents move up a level. Students see a folder once something in it is published.

**Announcements.** Post to a class on its **Announcements** page, and pin the important ones to the top. To post the same announcement to several classes at once, use the box on Home's **Activity** tab. Students see announcements on the class page and in their own Activity, next to newly published assignments.

**Calendar.** The **Calendar** in the sidebar shows every due date, discussion date and writing window across your classes (unpublished ones are marked, and only you see them). Filter it to one class, or switch to the list.

**Search.** **Search** in the sidebar, or Cmd+K (Ctrl+K on Windows and Chromebooks), jumps to any class, assignment, group project or student.

## 3. On the day

Students see what's due under **Coming up** on Home and on their Calendar (with anything overdue in red). They open the assignment, read the **Instructions** at the top of the page, and click **Start writing session**; the site's bar then slides away and the editor fills the window until the session ends. Then:

- Writing is only on while the Visor tab has focus. If a student leaves it, writing stops at once and the pause is recorded, with its length and reason. They see the same "Away 2m 14s" you see.
- With the extension, the Live tab says where a student went: **another tab**, **another Chrome window**, **another app**, **another desktop** or **minimized**. It also flags a second screen, developer tools, a page whose reports don't match the extension's, quick glances away, and typing that arrived while they were away. "Another desktop" means Chrome lost focus and Visor is hidden but not minimized, so a window that fully covers Visor reads the same.
- A locked session has no End session button. It ends when the student submits, when time runs out, or when you end it.
- Students see their essay only during a writing session. Outside one, it's hidden on the assignment, in the editor and in Download my data, until you return it. If you return it and reopen it for more writing, it's hidden again between sessions.
- Students can unsubmit until the due date passes or you review the essay, as long as they have time left. Once their time limit is used up they can't, and the page says why.
- The extension records their research (searches, pages and time on them, text copied), adds citations with **Cite this page** (or Alt+Shift+C), and in locked sessions keeps them on the allowed sites.
- In a locked session a student can click **Research in side panel**: they leave full screen and keep writing in Chrome's side panel while they browse. Blocked and AI sites stay blocked, every page is recorded, and time on other tabs counts as research time, not time away. Untick "Let students switch to the side panel" to keep a locked assignment in full screen.
- **Works Cited** decides where sources go: **a page after the essay** (built from what students cite; the default), **a side panel** next to the essay (not part of it, not printed), or **off** (no citation tools: students write their own Works Cited in the essay).
- Students add a source from a link, a DOI or an ISBN (Visor fills in what the page or the free Crossref and Open Library catalogues say, and tells them what's missing), by filling in the details, or by writing or pasting the whole citation. Untick "Let students cite a page in one click" to turn off only the extension's Cite button.
- Each source gets a **reliability grade**, A to D, that students see as they add it and next to it in their sources; you see the same grades on the review page. Click a grade to see what it's based on. Visor grades by the kind of site (a journal or government site, a news outlet, a website it doesn't know, a blog, social media, an AI chatbot…) and whether the source names an author and a date. It doesn't read the page or check its facts, and it uses no AI, so treat it as a starting point for students, not a verdict.

Home's **Live now** box lists every assignment with students writing right now. Open the assignment's **Live** tab to watch: status, time left, words, pastes, time away, and whether the extension and lockdown are on. **Needs attention** filters to students who've been away over a minute, lost the extension, visited an AI site, or pasted a big block from outside. You can pause, message, add time or end a session for one student or everyone.

Time is fair by design. Pausing a student (or everyone) stops their clock, so a fire drill costs nobody any writing time. A session window closes for everyone at the same moment, but extra time from **People**, and any time you add from the Live tab, carries past it. When time runs out or the window closes, **Time up** submits automatically if it's on.

## 4. Review

Students can **unsubmit** an essay until the due date, or until you save feedback or a score on it. It then leaves To review until they submit again, and the essay shows how many times they unsubmitted. They can't unsubmit once their time or the session window has run out, or if you ended the session and submitted for them.

## Your School

Every teacher has a **Your School** tab showing the school's students and teachers, with their roles and grades.

To set up a school, choose **Create a new school** when you sign up, or use the Your School tab. You become its **facilitator**, and a checklist takes you through the rest:

1. **Add your logo** under Settings.
2. **Add your people.** Paste email addresses, or copy columns straight from a spreadsheet, or upload a CSV. Name, role and grades are picked up from each line in any order, such as `Jo Park, jo@school.org, teacher, 9-10`. Anyone without a role or grade gets the defaults you choose, and you can fix any row before adding. Pasting a list again corrects what's there.
3. **Invite them.** Send invites by email, or copy each person's link if email isn't set up.

Once a school has a facilitator, it only takes people through invites. Picking its name isn't enough, and neither is signing up with an address on the list: the link is what proves it's them. Each link works for 30 days, for that one address. A student invited this way doesn't need to confirm they're 13 or older, because their school signed them up.

A student can't take themselves out of their school, in Profile or by accepting another school's invite. Their facilitator takes them off the list. In a school nobody runs yet, they stay until their teacher takes them out of its classes. Someone moving schools needs that first, and then the new school's invite works.

Facilitators also get a **Classes** tab: every class at the school, with its teachers, term and students. Click a class to choose who teaches it: change its owner, add a teacher, change their role, move them to another class, or take them off. It shows who's in a class, not their work. Only a class's own teachers can open it.

Facilitators can give a teacher a **title**, such as Head of English, under **Edit role, grades and title**. It shows with their name in Your School and in their classes.

A teacher can click **Ask to be a facilitator** on Your School. The request appears under Requests for the school's facilitators to approve or decline.

Select several people in the list to change their role or grades together, invite them, or remove them. Removing someone takes them out of the school; their account, classes and work stay. A school always keeps at least one facilitator who has joined, and a joined student can't be made a teacher here.

**Requests.** Anyone who isn't on your list can ask to join your school, from Home, from sign-up, or by trying a class code. Requests appear at the top of Your School. Approve one with a role and grades, or decline it. If they used a class code, approving puts them in that class too. Visor hasn't confirmed an email address unless it came from Google, and each request says which, so check before approving anyone as a teacher.

**Terms.** Terms are yours, not the school's. You start with the current school year; add your own, like Fall 2026, from **Terms** on Home or while creating a class. Every class belongs to your school, and sits in a term of whoever owns it. A class in a past term keeps working; it just moves off Home, into the term picker there.

**Who can be in a class.** Only people in the class's school. Students there join with the class code, or a teacher adds them. Someone on the list who hasn't joined yet is added as soon as they do. Anyone else is refused and can ask to join the school. Taking someone off the school's list takes them out of its classes too; their work stays with each class. Someone who owns a class has to archive it first.

A class from before classes needed a school keeps working as it did until a teacher adds it to their school, from the class page. Its students with no school come with it.

A school from before facilitators existed stays open to anyone until one of its teachers chooses **Become its facilitator**.

Home's **To review** box lists submitted work across your classes, oldest first. Or open an assignment's **Submissions** tab and any essay:

- **Document:** the essay and its Works Cited. **Color by source** marks what was typed, pasted from a page copied during the session, pasted from outside Visor's view, cited, or your starter text.
- **Replay:** watch the essay being written, with sessions, pauses and research marked on the timeline.
- **Process:** the numbers, and **Notes for the teacher**: plain facts like "Left the writing tab 7 times, 11 min in total" or "32% of the final text was pasted from outside Visor's view (3 pastes)."
- **Research:** every search, page, copy and citation, by session.
- **Feedback:** a comment and a score; **Return to student**, and tick "Let the student keep writing" to reopen it.

**Print / Save as PDF** prints the essay with an optional process summary page.

## What Visor won't tell you

Visor never says an essay was "written by AI" or gives a probability. It shows what happened (what was typed, what was pasted and from where, when the student was away) and leaves the judgment to you. It also can't see a second device (a phone); the IT guide (`extension/docs/IT-GUIDE.md`) covers what schools can lock down.

## Group projects and discussions

- **Group project:** make groups (drag students in, or "auto-make groups of N"), and paste each group's Google Doc link. Each group gets its own project: recordings of their meetings, Drive history, and the history the extension sends automatically from their Doc. Run the analysis there; the measured numbers come first, then a clearly labelled AI-written summary.
- **Discussion:** on the assignment's **Recordings** tab, confirm once that students know they're being recorded, then **Record in the browser** (long discussions are saved in 10-minute parts) or upload a file. Name the voices, then **Analyze** for each student's talk time, turns, words, who spoke after whom, and who didn't speak. You choose whether students see their own numbers.

## Privacy in one line

Research is kept 180 days (change it in the class's Settings), you can delete a student's work in a class at any time, and students can download all their data from Profile settings. Details: `/privacy`.
